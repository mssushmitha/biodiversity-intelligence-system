CLIMATE, LAND USE AND ENVIRONMENTAL CHANGE

Climate conditions strongly influence ecosystems, agriculture, water availability, and biodiversity. Important climate variables include temperature, rainfall, drought frequency, and seasonal variation.

Rainfall determines the amount of water available for plants, soil organisms, agricultural production, and wildlife. Low or irregular rainfall can increase water stress and reduce plant growth. Higher temperatures can increase evaporation and plant water demand.

Land use and land cover describe how land is occupied, such as forests, agricultural fields, grasslands, wetlands, and urban areas. Changes in land use can alter habitat availability and ecosystem functions.

Land-use change can cause habitat loss and fragmentation. When continuous natural habitats are divided into smaller isolated patches, movement and reproduction of some species can become more difficult.

Monoculture agriculture can reduce vegetation and habitat diversity compared with diversified agricultural systems. Intercropping, agroforestry, cover crops, hedgerows, and native vegetation can increase structural diversity in agricultural landscapes.

Important environmental relationships include:

1. Rainfall influences soil moisture and plant growth.
2. Temperature influences evaporation and water requirements.
3. Soil moisture influences plant survival and soil biodiversity.
4. Land-use change influences habitat availability and species richness.
5. Habitat fragmentation can reduce ecological connectivity.
6. Vegetation cover can protect soil from erosion and improve habitat conditions.

Important environmental metrics include:

- Annual rainfall
- Average temperature
- Soil moisture
- Vegetation cover
- Land-use composition
- Habitat connectivity
- Species richness
- Habitat diversity

For semi-arid agricultural regions, sustainable land management can include water conservation, diversified cropping, agroforestry, maintenance of vegetation buffers, and protection of natural habitat.
